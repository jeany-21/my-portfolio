LegalZoom Tax: Expert messaging

Designing a connected support experience for customers and tax experts.

My role

I joined the team when LegalZoom decided to build its own messaging tool from the ground up. As the sole designer, I led the end-to-end design of the new experience, from concept and research through testing and launch.

My role was to figure out how messaging could integrate into existing customer and expert workflows, while establishing a scalable foundation that could eventually extend across other LegalZoom services.

Impact

+75 NPS points

Improved customer satisfaction with the tax expert experience.

4× Faster Response

Reduced average response time from 2 days to 0.5 days through a more efficient workflow.

Scalable foundation

The messaging and collaboration patterns became reusable components in the global design library and were later incorporated into other legal workflows.

Problem: Customers couldn't get continuous support

LZ Tax is an online service that helps small business owners file their taxes with expert guidance throughout the process. Tax experts support customers along the way, from answering questions to reviewing their returns before filing.

Previously, customers and tax experts relied on email to communicate. Customers couldn't directly reach the expert working on their case, they had to email a general support address. This made it difficult to maintain context and continuity, often leaving customers feeling like they had to repeat themselves to get personalized support.

Challenge: Designing a seamless workflow across three roles

To provide a better support experience for customers, I needed to improve more than the customer experience. The service also depended on how tax experts and Operations worked behind the scenes. I started by researching and mapping how communication flowed across all three groups customers, tax experts, and Operations and understanding the needs of each.

Operations — Reduce manual triage

Operations needed a way to automatically route customer questions. Case-related questions could go directly to the expert already working on the case, while general questions could be assigned based on expert availability.

Customers — Build confidence and continuity

Customers wanted to know they were connected with an expert who understood their case. Keeping the conversation connected to the case meant they could receive more continuous, personalized support without repeatedly explaining their situation.

Experts — Reduce the friction of responding

Experts often had to move between different tools to find case information and communicate with customers. We wanted to bring the relevant context and communication together so experts could respond more efficiently.

Solution: One connected experience

A unified messaging experience integrated into both the customer and expert portals, creating a consistent, seamless way for customers and their tax experts to communicate throughout the filing process.

Operations — Smarter triage

Automatically route case-related questions to the expert already working on the case, while general questions can be assigned based on expert availability.

Customers — Build confidence and continuity

Connect customers with an expert who already understands their case, creating a more continuous and personalized support experience.

Experts — Everything in one place

Bring customer conversations directly into the case workflow, giving experts the context they need to respond without switching between tools.

Evernote task: Natural language due dates

Reducing friction in task management by letting users set due dates as they type.

My role

As the sole designer, I led the end-to-end design of the natural language due-date experience, partnering with Product and Engineering through concept, research, and usability testing.

Context

When it comes to task management, the ability to set a due date is essential. Users rely on due dates to plan their day and get reminders to complete tasks. As a result, setting a due date is the most-used feature in Evernote Tasks.

From a business perspective, due dates were also an important driver of engagement. Our data showed a strong correlation between due date usage and product engagement—the more due dates users set, the more active they were. Due date reminders also gave users a reason to return to Evernote and stay engaged.

Problem: Too many clicks to set a due date

Since due dates are such an important part of task management, even small amounts of friction can make the experience feel cumbersome.

In fact, one of the top reasons users stopped using Evernote Tasks was that setting a due date took too many clicks.

Users wanted to capture tasks as quickly as possible. While the existing flow took five clicks or fewer, it still felt like too much effort for a simple action.

Explorations

Explored different visual treatments and interaction patterns, using user testing to validate and refine the final direction.

Solution: Set due dates as you type

No clicks needed

Users can simply type a date into the task title using their keyboard, without interrupting their flow.

Seamless across devices

Smart Due Date was accessible from both the note and task modal, creating a consistent experience across mobile and desktop.

Understand how people naturally write dates

The system recognized 35+ common ways of writing dates, allowing users to express due dates naturally without learning a specific format.

Impact

Reduced user clicks, improving overall task completion time.

Laid the foundation for adding more natural language processing features.

LZ Books: New native app experience

Reimagining financial management for mobile.

My role

As the sole designer working on this app, I led the design from concept to launch, conducted post-launch user research, established the design system foundation, and partnered closely with Product, Engineering, and other cross-functional teams.

Impact

34%

of existing web users adopted the app within ~2 months of launch.

3.9× increase

in users reviewing transactions via mobile.

Context

LZ Books is a financial management tool for small business owners. It helps them manage everyday finances, from tracking transactions and discovering deductions to creating and sending invoices.

At the time, LZ Books was only available on the web, while users were increasingly accessing the product from their phones.

Problem: Web-only limited financial management on the go

Research showed that users relied on the web for deeper tasks, like reviewing their business and analyzing financial insights. But quick, everyday tasks were much harder to accomplish away from a computer.

Something as simple as checking a transaction or sending an invoice required users to open a browser, navigate to LZ Books, and log in before they could get started.

25% of users were already accessing LZ Books through mobile web, signaling an opportunity to make these everyday financial tasks faster and more accessible on mobile.

Challenge

Prioritize what belongs on mobile

With a limited timeline, we couldn't simply bring every web feature into the app. I worked closely with PM and Engineering to define the roadmap, using product data and user research to identify which workflows would deliver the most value on mobile. The goal was to balance user needs, business priorities, and technical feasibility while focusing on experiences where mobile could provide a meaningful advantage.

Establishing the foundation for a scalable design system

As we introduced new mobile experiences, I also needed to establish a scalable design system alongside the product. Rather than designing each feature in isolation, I looked for reusable patterns and components that could support future workflows. This meant considering how patterns would scale across different use cases, defining component behaviors and states, and creating a foundation the team could continue building on after launch.

Solution: A mobile-first experience for everyday finances

Leverage native capabilities to make everyday financial tasks more seamless

From a Live Activity that keeps mileage tracking visible to native sharing options that let users send invoices beyond email, mobile unlocked faster, more seamless ways to get things done.

Adapt familiar mobile interaction patterns instead of directly transferring the web experience to mobile

I rethought workflows around the constraints and behaviors of a smaller screen—for example, replacing long horizontal tables with more natural mobile interactions, and using gestures like swipe to make reviewing transactions faster.

Build a design system that supports both light and dark mode
